Problem statement

Farmers often struggle to identify crop diseases early because symptoms look similar and expert advice is not always available. Manual identification is slow and can lead to wrong decisions.

This project uses deep learning to analyse a leaf image, predict the disease class learned during training, and show basic informational guidance.

Objectives

  1. Train a deep learning model for leaf disease classification.
  2. Use a documented dataset with proper train/validation/test splits.
  3. Build a simple, responsive web application.
  4. Show the predicted disease, confidence and precautions.
  5. Evaluate with accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and confusion matrix.

How to use

  1. Take a close, well-lit photo of a single leaf on a plain background.
  2. Open the Predict tab and upload the image (JPG, JPEG or PNG, max 8 MB).
  3. Press Predict Disease and read the result, confidence and guidance.
  4. Press Upload another image to test another leaf.

Supported crops and diseases

Supported classes are exactly those the model was trained and evaluated on (stored in models/class_indices.json); the app lists them from that file. No disease is claimed unless it was trained and tested.

Methodology

  1. Load dataset, inspect class distribution.
  2. Remove corrupted and duplicate images.
  3. Split into train / validation / test (duplicates kept in one split to avoid leakage).
  4. Augment training images only (flip, rotation, zoom, contrast).
  5. Train MobileNetV2 (frozen base, then fine-tune); CNN baseline for comparison.
  6. Select the model on validation loss; evaluate once on the test set.

Prediction logic

The saved model outputs a probability for each class. The highest one is shown as the prediction and its probability as model confidence, which is not a guarantee of correctness.

If the confidence is low or the top two classes are close, the app shows an uncertain warning instead of a diagnosis. This heuristic cannot catch every unsupported image.

Disease guide general educational information

No product names or doses are given. Confirm any treatment with a local agricultural expert.

    Limitations

    • Only the trained classes are supported.
    • PlantVillage images are lab-style; accuracy on real field photos is usually lower.
    • A leaf photo alone may not reveal the true cause (nutrient, water stress, pests).
